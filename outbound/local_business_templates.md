businesses (restaurants, salons, contractors, shops) that show up on maps with a
phone number but **no website**. Different audience from agencies: these are
busy owners, not peers. They don't care about your stack. They care about
*more customers* and *looking legit on Google*.

**Rules**
- Most of these have a **phone, not an email** — so phone/text is your main channel.
- Fill `[BLANKS]` per business. `[BIZ]` = business name. `[SPECIFIC]` = one true,
  concrete detail (their food, their service, a real review you saw, the
  neighborhood). Never send/say a blank.
- Lead with *them*. Mention you found them on Google/Maps — that's the natural,
  honest reason you're reaching out, and it sets up the hook.
- Don't insult them for not having a site. Frame it as opportunity, not a flaw.
- Offer something concrete and low-risk (a free quick mockup beats "let's chat").

### Tips that actually move the needle
- **The free mockup is your edge.** Showing > telling. Even a one-page draft makes
  this real and separates you from every "I can build you a website" spammer.
- **Call mid-morning or mid-afternoon**, not during their rush (avoid lunch/dinner
  for restaurants, mornings for salons).
- **Texting from a real local number** gets far higher response than email here.
- **Log every touch in `tracker.csv`** so you don't double-contact or lose a warm one.
- **Compliance:** for SMS, identify yourself, keep it low-volume and personal (not
  bulk-blasted), and stop if they ask. US TCPA rules are stricter for automated/bulk
  texting — sending these one-by-one from your own phone as genuine individual
  outreach is the safe way to start.
